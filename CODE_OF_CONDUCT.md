# Code of Conduct

This repository is intended for rigorous technical criticism and open research.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- address arguments, evidence, methods, and artifacts rather than attacking people;
- make disagreements as specific and falsifiable as practical;
- welcome corrections, counterexamples, null results, failed replications, and prior art;
- distinguish established evidence from inference or speculation;
- represent the framework's proof status accurately;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest when they materially affect a contribution;
- respect privacy and do not publish another person's private information without permission.

## Unacceptable conduct

Do not use this repository for:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or discriminatory abuse;
- doxxing or publication of private personal information;
- deliberate impersonation or deceptive attribution;
- spam, repetitive promotion, or unrelated solicitation;
- knowingly fabricated citations, data, experiments, or quotations;
- persistent disruption after a maintainer has asked that conduct stop.

Strong criticism of the framework, its author, its premises, or its conclusions is not a
conduct violation merely because it is negative. Substantive disagreement is welcome.

## Research-status integrity

The current framework status is:

> **Stage 4 — candidate proof architecture under named premises, without independent
> specialist verification and without theorem closure.**

Misstating that status accidentally should be corrected. Repeatedly misrepresenting the
archive after correction may be treated as disruptive behavior.

Specialist handoff files are working verification materials, not proof certificates.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock, close, or remove contributions that violate this code and
may restrict participation when necessary.

For a conduct concern that should not be filed publicly, use the contact route listed at:

https://alignmentconstraint.org/core/for-researchers/

Reports should include enough information to evaluate the concern while minimizing
unnecessary personal information.
